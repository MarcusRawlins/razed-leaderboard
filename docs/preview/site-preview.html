Our story

It started with a violin and a weird question.

The idea

Everything has a rhythm.

It’s an old idea: everything, down to the atom, is energy in motion, and living things fall into step with the rhythms around them. We wanted to make that idea useful.

We pictured a belt, a T-shirt, a pendant and a bottle of shampoo, each carrying the same music. Then we spent two decades building them.

The music

The first product was an album.

Co-developer Robert Anthony Aviles, a composer on 7-string electric violin, turned the measurements into music worth listening to. Rhythm of the Light came out in 1998, after an early trial in a hospital emergency department.

Here he plays one of those early pieces, Tibet, live.

Tibet, performed live by Robert Anthony Aviles

Our mission

Turn down the noise. Turn up the music.

Today most of our work happens behind other people’s brands, in products made by companies, clinics and households around the world. We license the technology for consumables, cosmetics, apparel and jewelry, and we sell our own kits and the Chromatones membership directly.

Helping people worldwide, one person at a time, since 1998.

The science

How we turn a song into stuff.

Your body has a rhythm. We wrote it a song. Then we spent 25 years figuring out how to fit that song into a pendant, a drop of water and a phone sticker. Here are the liner notes.

Step one

Write the song.

We measured how molecules bond and pulse, turned those measurements into ratios, and set the ratios to the twelve-note scale. The result was short phrases we call Sonic Scripts. Performed together on real instruments, they make one composition: the Sonic Formula.

Step two

Unplug the speakers.

Songs end. Ours doesn’t have to. From 2003 on, we found ways to lock it inside objects, so it plays all day without a single speaker.

Go deeper

The deep cut.

For the nerds. We love you.

The biofield+

Every living thing gives off a faint electromagnetic field. Researchers in complementary medicine call it the biofield. Our work starts from one premise: that field has a balanced, healthy shape, and it tends to drift when life gets loud.

From geometry to music+

We took measurements of how molecules bond and pulse and expressed them as ratios. Mapped onto the twelve-note musical scale, each ratio became a short phrase. We call these phrases Sonic Scripts.

Layering rhythm, melody and harmony+

Sonic Scripts were layered the way a composer layers parts: rhythm first, then melody, then harmony. Performed on real instruments, they became full compositions. Together they make the Sonic Formula, and the recording is what every product carries.

Why entrainment+

Entrainment is the tendency of rhythms to fall into step, like pendulum clocks on a shared wall or a crowd clapping together. Harmonic Energy is built on the idea that a field near a steady pattern tends to settle toward it. It is a premise we test, not a law of physics we claim.
