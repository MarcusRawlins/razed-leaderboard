## Compliance flags for the rebrand

The site makes health and scientific claims that the FTC requires to be backed by competent, reliable scientific evidence, and its own disclaimer says the approach is not FDA-accepted. A disclaimer does not cancel claims made elsewhere on the page. Have the client's counsel review these before any copy carries over.

- **Disease-adjacent claims:** Chromatones offers "remedies" for "a specific condition"; Validation lists inflammation, blood pressure, blood toxicity, hearing and brain-disorder testing.
- **Implied clinical backing:** "university reviews", "clinical studies", "hospital" testing, and "unequivocally" consistent results. The named study leads are the North American licensee's CEO and an author, i.e. not independent.
- **Scale claims without source:** "millions" of users (Home, About), "hundreds of thousands" (Validation).
- **Physics claims:** EMF shielding/neutralizing "Electrosmog", quantum entanglement between products, NIH attribution of the term "living biofield" (the NIH Office of Alternative Medicine did use "biofield", but the surrounding claims are the company's).
- **Undisclosed integration:** Shopping says some partners add the technology "in secret". Worth deciding deliberately whether the new brand repeats this.
- **Gift / free offer:** "You'll get them all, for free" (monthly Wave Riders) needs clear terms if kept.

For positioning, the safer pattern is experience and craft language (sound, music, ritual, the Aviles story) over mechanism and outcome claims.

## Rebuild audit

First-pass recommendations from the crawl. Items marked *verify* need a browser or client access to confirm.

### Security

**Found in the crawl**

| Finding | Risk | Fix on the new site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cookie Accept/Decline buttons link to nothing; analytics likely runs regardless of choice | Consent not honored (GDPR/CCPA exposure if any EU/CA visitors) | Real consent manager that blocks tags until accepted |
| No privacy policy or terms, yet cookies, a login and forms exist | Legal exposure; required by most payment processors and ad platforms | Privacy, terms, refund and shipping pages before launch |
| /login in the main menu; purpose unknown (*verify*) | Advertises an attack surface; unknown what it protects | Move member login off the public nav; confirm MFA and rate limiting on the new platform |
| Platform and version exposed in page metadata (GoDaddy Website Builder 8.0) | Fingerprinting | Strip generator tags on the new build |
| Q3 page embeds a broken javascript: srcdoc reference | Sloppy code; may be a leftover third-party embed | Remove or rebuild Q3 |
| Contact page shows no form in markup (*verify*) | Unknown where submissions go | Form with spam protection, TLS, and a known inbox |
| Health claims site-wide (see Compliance flags) | Regulatory risk, not technical | Legal review before migration |

**Passive checks to run next** (non-intrusive, any public site): HTTPS redirect and certificate, security headers (HSTS, CSP, X-Frame-Options, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy), third-party scripts loaded, cookie flags (Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite), DNS and registrar lock, email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), exposed files (/sitemap.xml, /robots.txt, backups), and a check of the Vimeo privacy settings on each video.

**Needs written authorization first:** vulnerability scanning, login testing, form fuzzing, or anything that sends unusual traffic. Get a signed scope letter from the client naming the domain, dates and tester before running any of it, even though they own the site.

### Speed and technical

- **Images:** almost every in-body image has an empty source. Either the builder is lazy-loading via script (slow, invisible to crawlers) or the images are missing. Collect originals from the client; serve as WebP/AVIF with explicit dimensions and alt text.
- **Video:** the Home hero is a Vimeo video; Technologies loads four Vimeo players at once. Use a poster image and load players on click, or self-host short loops.
- **Markup bloat:** the 13-item menu is repeated three or four times per page. One menu, rendered once.
- **Technologies page:** \~4,000 words with half duplicated. Cut the duplicate and split into tabs or sub-pages.
- **Platform:** GoDaddy Website Builder limits caching, image optimization, structured data and checkout. Any modern platform (Webflow, Shopify, WordPress, Framer) is an upgrade; pick based on the Checkout section.
- **Measure before and after:** run PageSpeed Insights / Lighthouse on the current Home, Technologies and a kit page to set a baseline for the report (*verify*).

### Checkout and conversion

There is no checkout. Nothing on the site can be bought: no prices, no cart, no payment, no kit order form. Shopping sends visitors to third-party stores, Chromatones has no signup, and the kit pages have no buttons.

**Decide first with the client**

1. Does LésAvi sell direct, or only through licensees? The answer sets the platform.
2. If direct: which products (sample kits, Chromatones subscription, Wave Riders), at what prices, shipping where, with what fulfilment.
3. If licensee-only: Shopping becomes a partner directory with clear tracking links, and the kits become a lead form for B2B samples.

**If selling direct, the build needs**

- Product catalog with the naming system cleaned up (see kit pages) and real photos
- Subscription billing for Chromatones (monthly, with the free Wave Rider fulfilment tracked)
- Tax, shipping and returns policies
- Order and account emails; the current site sends none
- Payment processor onboarding, which will ask about the health claims. Processors routinely refuse or hold funds for wellness products with medical-sounding claims, so the Compliance flags section affects whether checkout can exist at all.

**Conversion basics missing today:** no testimonials with names, no FAQ, no trust signals (guarantee, shipping time, contact), no email capture outside the quiz.
